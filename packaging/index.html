Confirm before print

  • "24g Protein" on the sachet. This comes from your sample. A 30 g sachet giving 24 g of protein would be about 80% protein by weight, which a wheat and soy blend is unlikely to reach. Check whether 24 g means three rotis' worth, and what exactly the claim refers to.
  • "3x Protein" and "All 9 essential amino acids". Both are claims. They need lab results and a regulatory check (FSSAI claims rules) before they go on a pack.
  • Nutrition table, ingredient percentages, dose and shelf life are blank on purpose. Nothing is invented.
  • Legal block: FSSAI licence number, manufacturer and marketer address, MRP, batch, mfg and best-before, customer care, barcode and veg mark. Placeholders are marked in square brackets.
  • Allergen line reads wheat (gluten) and soy, as you confirmed no peanut. Add a "may contain" line if the factory handles peanut.

Design choices

  • One system, two sizes. Sage upper half with the logo, a cream lower half for claims and benefits. The sachet matches your sample; the pouch adds the headline "Same roti. 3x protein." and a 500 g badge.
  • Real product photo. The wheat and soy photo sits on the colour split, as on the website.
  • Readable at a glance. Brand, then claim, then benefit chips, then the how-to line. Fine print is on the back.
  • Pouch details. Zip groove, tear notches and crimped seals are drawn in the mockup so you can judge the look. They are not part of the artwork files.
  • Logo note. The logo's brown "Grain" is a little soft on sage. A cream or knocked-out logo version would read better. Worth testing on a printed proof.

Next steps

  • Pick the packaging printer and ask for the exact dielines (sachet and zip pouch).
  • Confirm the claims, dose and nutrition numbers with the lab and your FSSAI consultant.
  • After any text change, re-run python3 packaging/build.py from the project folder to rebuild every file on this page.
  • Order a short digital proof run for both packs before the full print.
